The appraisal system changes
Nov 2, 2026. Protect your deals.

Every residential appraisal form you've ever seen on a transaction is being replaced by a single new digital report (the redesigned "dynamic" URAR, built on the new UAD 3.6 data standard). Most of it is behind-the-scenes — but a couple of pieces land right on your closing dates. Here's the plain-English version.
NOV 2, 2026
Mandatory for conventional loans (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac). After this date, appraisals on the old standard are rejected — no grace period. FHA, VA & USDA are moving on their own separate timelines.

✔ What does NOT change

Your client's home value. A licensed appraiser still determines value the same professional way — same comps, same adjustments. Only how the report is formatted and delivered is changing. If anyone says a home is worth more or less "because of the new rule," they're wrong.

⚠ What to expect at first

During the transition, expect possibly longer appraisal turn times and potentially higher fees as appraisers learn new software and do more on-site data collection. The industry expects this to be temporary — and to speed up once everyone's on the new system.

What you should do on every file closing this fall

  • Write longer appraisal contingency windows. Where you'd normally do 14–21 days, have the conversation up front.
  • Order the appraisal early. Waiting on inspections first is about to get expensive on the calendar.
  • Prep your sellers for a longer contract timeline if the buyer is financing — talk timing at the listing appointment.
  • Ask your lender one question every time: "Which appraisal standard is this on, and what's your current turn time?"
